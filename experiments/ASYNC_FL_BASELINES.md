# Asynchronous baselines in the two-protocol study

The fixed-ten-round comparison uses FedAvg, Fixed-25/50/75 and SplitFleet only. The separate common-wall-clock comparison adds FedAsync, FedBuff and FedCompass. Both protocols have four full task models and five original paired seeds, with independent 100/160-job populations under `results/fl_dual_protocol_20261010/`.

FedAsync implements stale-model interpolation from [Xie et al., Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1903.03934v5): mixing `0.9 * (1 + delay)**-0.5`, with a local proximal penalty of `0.005 / 2 * ||w - w_dispatch||²`. FedBuff implements the mean of two completed parameter deltas from [Nguyen et al., Algorithms 1–2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/nguyen22b.html), with server learning rate 1. The authors' [FLSim aggregation implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/FLSim/blob/main/flsim/optimizers/async_aggregators.py) applies the aggregated gradient through an optimizer over model parameters. This harness averages floating non-parameter states uniformly across the two completed contributions and preserves typed counters; running statistics are not optimizer deltas. FedBuff does not flush an incomplete buffer into the endpoint at the deadline. Secure aggregation and differential privacy are not implemented.

Both new baselines use constant local steps 10/80/40/20 for ResNet/BERT/RF-DETR/DeepLab. These approximate one mean-partition epoch and match FedCompass's existing maximum steps; they are not tuned on new performance outcomes. FedAsync uses uniform per-arrival interpolation; FedBuff averages deltas uniformly. FedCompass retains its sample-size-weighted delta aggregation and adaptive grouping, documented in `FEDCOMPASS_BASELINE.md`. All methods use common task-specific Adam settings, with optimizers recreated per round/dispatch. These optimizer, buffer and small-device choices delimit the mechanism adaptation; they do not reproduce the complete original systems or establish their theoretical guarantees.

The new mechanisms reuse the physical Flower population binding, one outstanding RPC per client, exact cyclic-loader step execution, initial readiness barrier, evaluation cadence and drain from the FedCompass harness. `async_fl_validation.py` checks partition isolation, logical clients/devices/processes, step/tail sample counts, proximal coefficients, buffer membership, model versions, staleness, commit timestamps, failures and metrics. Communication counts all completed training application buffers, including discarded/drained assignments, with the same declared exclusions for every method.

Admission regression completed 72 CPU and actual local RPC tests. Ten actual-device integration jobs exercise the two new asynchronous methods on all four tasks plus DeepLab Fixed-25/SplitFleet. Their finite states, valid source/population/work/readiness receipts and at least one committed model update are launch requirements; pilot accuracy is not a selection criterion. Pilot and historical outcomes are excluded from the new primary populations.
